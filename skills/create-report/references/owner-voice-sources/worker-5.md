## Per-context profile

### Notes (class notes, bullet-point, self-facing)
Register: informal, telegraphic, written for herself, not an audience. Frequent editorializing breaks into an otherwise dry outline.

Sentence shape: mostly sentence fragments and noun phrases as bullets (no verb, no subject: "Bring these into class", "Turn off IPV6"). Full sentences appear only when she's annoyed or explaining something to future-self. Fragments outnumber full sentences roughly 3:1.

Word choice: no hedges in notes (declarative even when wrong), heavy abbreviation (`w/`, `+`, `eg.`, `yr`), contractions used freely (`don't`, `it's`, `you're`). Recurring intensifier: "very" and "really." Sarcasm shows up as a flat aside dropped into a factual list with no transition.

Structure: ALL-CAPS for section headers or reminders ("QUIZ NEXT WEEK", "SHOW FISMO ROLES"). Nested nested bullets, no numbering. No intros or conclusions — notes start mid-topic and stop when the lecture stops (several files trail off after one line).

Punctuation/spelling: consistent misspelling of acronyms under time pressure ("FISMO" for FSMO, repeated across two files — not a one-off typo, a memorized-wrong term). Curly quotes from Word autocorrect. Missing periods on fragment bullets, but sentences get periods. Comma splices when she's writing fast ("Turn off IPV6 / Manually set IPV4 addresses" — bullet, no comma issue, but full-sentence asides run commas together).

## Cross-cutting observations

Constant across every context: contractions when the register allows them at all (never suppressed except in formal reports), a habit of naming what she doesn't know rather than bluffing, and a preference for short declaratives even inside longer paragraphs.

What shifts: sarcasm and personal asides are present only in notes and reflective writing, never in formal reports or technical answers — she code-switches hard between "talking to herself/a peer" and "talking to an institution." Sentence length scales with formality (notes < technical answers < reflective prose < formal reports). Fragments are load-bearing in notes (default mode) but rhetorical (deliberate, rare) in reflective prose, and absent in formal reports.

What marks a piece as hers vs. generic: the sarcastic one-liner dropped mid-list with no setup ("Not everything is on fire," "China needs to chill out," "It's actually garbage") is the strongest fingerprint — several files with no name header were attributed to her on this signal alone (Discussion 5, JPNE111_FinalPresentationNotes, MidtermNotes). Generic/not-hers files (lab templates, the "Final Sample," the map printout) read as flat procedural imperative or third-party polish with zero first-person intrusion and no admitted uncertainty — that absence is itself diagnostic.

Two pairs of near-duplicate files exist (LEGL-320 Assignment 4 in two saves; IA-343 Chinese Info Warfare paper as draft + final) — same authorial voice, final versions tightened but didn't change register.
