## Cross-cutting observations

Constant across every context except LEGL320 and the Final Report: contractions, first-person voice, and a habit of narrating her own reasoning in real time ("I thought X, however that was not the issue, next I thought Y"). This step-by-step narration is the strongest authorship fingerprint — it appears identically in technical assignments, personal essays, and code comments.

Sentence-opener habit: "So," "However," and "I" as paragraph/sentence starters recur across technical, personal, and code contexts.

Recurring misspellings/slips across contexts (distinct fingerprint, not context-bound): "image" for "imagine," "invintory" for "inventory," "seperate" for "separate" (IA462 Assignment 2), "sataus" for "status" and "Cival" for "Civil" (LEGL320), "ciperher" for "cipher" (Lab-3_SSL-TLS), "queues" for "cues" (COMM-225), "presents" for "presence" (PP Website doc), "Expecation"/"Profesor" in filenames and body text (IA-495).

What shifts: formality tracks the audience, not the topic. LEGL320 (graded, evaluative, no personal stake) and the IA473 Final Report (client-facing deliverable) drop contractions and first-person almost entirely. Everything she treats as "showing my own work" — technical lab writeups, COMM reflections, personal essay, code comments, status updates — keeps the contracted, first-person, self-narrating voice even when the assignment is formally structured (headers, numbered sections).

Notes vs. prose: her notes (Bluetooth, IA-473, STAT-170) are compressed outlines carrying almost no personal voice on their own — they read as transcribed/reorganized source material. Her personal annotations inside otherwise-neutral material (Practice Prob Chapt 2's "#" comments) are the clearest injection of voice into notes, and the "#" marker for an aside matches her Perl commenting habit.

What marks a piece as hers vs. generic: presence of first-person narrated reasoning, a sentence-initial "So/However," a self-deprecating or dryly opinionated aside, and the recurring typo set above. Absence of all four (pure fact lists, no narration, clean spelling, no personal pronoun) is a signal a document is either instructor-provided or a low-content procedural fragment.
