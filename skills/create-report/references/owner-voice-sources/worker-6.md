## Cross-cutting observations

- **Semicolon-as-colon glossary pattern** recurs across three otherwise unrelated contexts (Research Journal, Presentation Skeleton, Udemy Notes): `Term; short definition`. This is a real habit, not noise from one document.
- **Hedging vocabulary is consistent everywhere she is uncertain**: "not sure", "I believe", "maybe", "might" — present in notes, present even inside the otherwise-polished Draft-1 proposal, absent from the later mentor-edited drafts (evidence the later drafts are less purely her own wording).
- **Intensifier stacking** ("really", "so", "super", "very", "incredibly") is strongest in personal reflection, present but muted in notes, essentially absent in the formal report register.
- **Contractions**: used freely in notes, assignments, and personal reflection; suppressed in the formal proposal/report register — a genuine register switch, not an inconsistency.
- **Comma splices and run-on sentences with "and" chains** are a durable habit that surfaces even under pressure (Journal) and in her most polished writing (Draft-1's proposal prose still runs long, comma-linked sentences).
- **Deliberate humor survives inside required, graded work**: the running gag placeholder signature in MGMT-202, the repeated ironic "Facebook" branding memo, the aside "or whoever usually writes this stuff." This is a distinguishing marker of authenticity — copied/templated material never carries jokes like this.
- **What marks a document as generic/not-hers-in-spirit vs. genuinely hers**: documents that are just a copied source (Objectives_Vocabulary.docx, most of Chapter 10 Company Search) read as flat aggregation with citation blocks and none of the above tics — no hedges, no intensifiers, no comma splices, no jokes. The presence of even one hedge, aside, or joke is a reliable "this is her, not a copy-paste" signal.
- **Typos cluster with fatigue/pace**, not randomly — the longest, latest-in-session journal entries carry the most doubled/dropped letters ("opernair", "Reperformed", "receed"), while short assignments and headers are typo-free.
- **Two-pass composition habit** (prose paragraph draft, then bulleted outline restating the same points) shows up once, in In Circles — worth noting as a possible general drafting habit if seen again in other slices.
