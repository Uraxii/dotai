# When you are refused

Full mode. Load the moment a document cannot be obtained.

Access failures are normal and they are findings. ATP 2-22.9's collection
chapter treats an unavailable source as an outcome reported back against the
requirement, not a silent hole. Three kinds, three responses.

**The host refuses the client.** Page a browser serves and a script does not.
Try the same document from another host carrying it: another arm of the same
organization, a national archive, a library mirror. Copy taken instead of the
original -> record which host served it and what the original did, so the
reader can weigh it. No standard here says when a copy is good enough, and this
skill does not invent one.

**The document is behind a wall.** Paywalled, login-gated, or licensed. Do not
route around the wall. Cite the document by title and section from what you can
legitimately see, mark in its row that the text is not stored, and record in
item 7 that the claim rests on material you could not keep.

**The document does not exist, or nobody will give it to you.** Say so in item
7, name what you asked for and of whom, and say what the answer rests on
instead. Refusal recorded is evidence about the subject. Refusal silently
dropped turns an incomplete collection into one that looks complete.

Every one of these gets a consulted-log line whatever happens.
