# Collection: fixing the question, planning, and finding

Steps 1 to 3. Load at step 1, keep open until first document stored.

## Fix the question

Area of interest is not a question. "Whether to acquire the supplier" = area.
"Does the supplier's order book survive the loss of its largest customer" =
question, because something would answer it.

Three tests a question must pass before collection starts. From GAO-12-208G,
*Designing Evaluations*, chapter 2, written for this exact moment.

- **Specific.** Names the thing, the period, the population. Question askable
  of any company in any year gets answered that way.
- **Measurable.** Some observable answers it. Cannot name one -> topic, not
  question.
- **Free of its own answer.** "Why is the rollout failing" presumes failure.
  Ask whether it is failing, then why.

Somebody else set the question -> record their exact wording beside your
restatement, settle the difference with them before working. FM 5-0's staff
study chapter puts it as a step with teeth: problem handed to you gets approved
before you act on it. Delegated agent that cannot reach the requester records
both wordings, marks the difference, proceeds; claims no approval it never got.

Do not write the answer down yet, even provisionally. Answer is what the
collection turns out to support.

## Plan the collection

Full mode. Two lists, written before the first document is opened.

**What would answer it.** Per candidate answer, name the observable that would
establish it. Not a topic to read about: a thing that either is or is not the
case.

**What would break it.** Per candidate answer, name what you would expect to
see if it were true and are not seeing, and what you would expect to see if it
were false. This is the half agents skip; skipping it is how a collection ends
up holding only material that fits.

Then order the work. GAO-20-195G, the *GAO Cost Estimating and Assessment
Guide*, chapter 9, gives the sequence that survives contact: settle purpose,
scope, and what you measure against first, then plan the collection, then
collect, revising the plan as findings change it. Source preference:
references/sources.md.

ATP 2-22.9, *Open-Source Intelligence*, chapter 1, adds the discipline keeping
a plan honest: turn the question into named requirements, decide which source
could satisfy each, keep assessing whether the collection satisfies them rather
than merely running.

Two ordering rules worth following.

- Go after what discriminates first. Document fitting every candidate answer
  equally well told you nothing, however solid.
- Go after what expires first. Person leaving, log with a retention window,
  page that changes weekly.

## Find candidates

Gather the candidate list before examining any of it in detail.

Record where each candidate came from at the moment you find it.

Vary where you look, not just how much. Search engine, official register,
originator's own publications, regulator's filings, person who was there return
different material by construction, not different amounts of the same material.
ATP 2-22.9, *Open-Source Intelligence*, chapter 3, puts it as a survey step:
work out what kinds of source exist for this question before working through any
one of them, and treat the survey as a product in its own right.

Candidate you cannot obtain is a finding, not a hole. references/refused.md.
